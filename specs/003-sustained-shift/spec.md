confirmation review, with its own hash, seeds and thresholds (constitution III). Spec 001,
`sim/PREREG.md`, the frozen results, `sim/ATTRIBUTION*` and paper v4 are unchanged.

Base: spec 001 sections 3 to 5 (generator, architectures, metrics), as amended by sections 11 to 15. This
spec states only what differs.

Target: spec 001 section 15, a sustained shift in the mean level of `D`, decided 2026-10-03 under his
delegation.

Framing: section 0's ruling and D5's guards hold unchanged.
- `D` is one invented, non-diagnostic wellbeing/functioning state.
- No condition is named, and nothing is screened.
- Every number is synthetic (constitution II, VI-b).

**Design evidence.** `sim/design_check_003.py` writes `sim/design_check_003.json`. This is a design-stage
check: its numbers size the choices below, but they are not results and they score no prediction.
- Seed 20261003, which is neither the tuning seed nor a PREREG-derived seed.
- It imports `drift_sim.py` unchanged and patches only `DELTA_D`.
- Cell: spec 001's default, with constant help 0.3.
- Days: detection runs on days 0 to 209, and `D` continues for 87 more days for the confirmation
  probes.
- Onsets U[50, 100]; 1000 persons per run.
- A-fixed, with `h` from mixed sources: the frozen PREREG value at `phi_D = 0.7`, and the robustness
  retunes at 0.3 and 0.9.

## 1. Questions

- **Q1. Detection.** How well does a passive within-person score detect a sustained mean shift of at
  least `delta_min` across the `phi_D` grid, at the frozen false-alarm target?
- **Q2. Confirmation.** Do confirmation probes spaced past the state's memory separate sustained shifts
  from transient excursions? What do they cost in days to confirmation, and what part of the remaining
  error comes from the baseline?
- **Q3. Person versus observer.** Does a policy log separate person change from observer change where
  indicators and probes do not (spec 001 sections 11.5 and 14)?

No method has to win (spec 001 section 11.5). A rule that fails is reported as failing.

## 2. Target scoring

A family's status is set by the generator, never by the detector.

| status | generator condition | alarms count as |
|---|---|---|
| positive | a deterministic mean component of `D` of at least `delta_min`, held for at least 14 days after the ramp ends (spec 001 section 15) | detections inside the window; false alarms before onset |
| null | no deterministic mean component; AR(1) excursions of any length | false alarms |
| sub-threshold | a deterministic component in (0, `delta_min`) | their own rate after onset, outside both FA and sensitivity; false alarms before onset |

A component of at least `delta_min` held for less than 14 days is generated by no family in this study.

The reference for "shift" is the person's generator mean before onset, which is 0. It is not the
baseline window's estimate. The detector sees only that estimate, so baseline error is a false-alarm
source this study measures (sections 6 and 7). It is not part of the target.

## 3. `delta_D`: threshold and generator grid

**`delta_min = 1.0` SD of `D`.** Generator shifts are **{0.5 sub-threshold, 1.0, 1.5}**. Composite shift
per unit of `delta_D` is 0.343 to 0.354 at 1.0 across `phi_D`, and linear across the design check's grid.

| value | role | why |
|---|---|---|
| 1.0 | threshold, and the hardest positive | **(a) Probe resolution.** At reliability 0.7, `Var(e) = 0.429`. A contrast of 3 spaced probes against a 4-probe baseline block has SD `sqrt(1.429/3 + 1.429/4) = 0.913` with independent probes. At margin `delta/2`, a 1.0 shift is confirmed with probability 0.71 and a null with 0.29. At 0.5 the figures are 0.61 and 0.39. More confirmation probes cannot close that gap: with 4 baseline probes the contrast SD never falls below `sqrt(1.429/4) = 0.598`, so a null is confirmed at margin 0.25 with probability at least 0.34. **(b) Baseline error.** At `phi_D = 0.7` the SD of the true 28-day baseline mean of `D` is 0.413 (design check), so 1.0 is about 2.4 baseline-error SDs and 0.5 about 1.2. At 0.9 that SD is 0.671, and even 1.0 is only 1.5 SDs; section 6 exists because of this. **(c) Comparability.** The frozen DR uses 1.0. |
| 1.5 | clearly positive | The first grid value where the frozen CUSUM has positive expected drift: the composite shift of 0.519 at `phi_D = 0.7` exceeds `k = 0.5`. A-fixed 21-day sensitivity is 0.292, against 0.186 at 1.0, with 292 against 186 detected persons. That is enough alarms to compare attribution rules. |
| 0.5 | sub-threshold | How often the layer fires on a shift below the target. 21-day sensitivity is 0.096, against a chance level of 0.059 in the same check (pseudo-onsets on S, at `phi_D = 0.7`). |

The threshold is a resolution limit of this measurement design. It is not a claim that a 1-SD change
matters, or does not matter, for any person, and no clinical anchor is used.

**The frozen `k` is mismatched to this target.**
- The CUSUM reference value is conventionally half the shift to be detected. The CUSUM is Page 1954,
  *Biometrika* 41(1-2):100-115, metadata checked on Crossref. The half-shift convention is textbook SPC
  and was not checked against a source here.
- `k = 0.5` therefore suits a composite shift of 1.0, which needs `delta_D` of about 2.9.
- At `delta_min` the composite shift (0.35) is below `k`. Frozen detection at 1.0 therefore depends on
  `D`'s excursions on top of the shift. This explains part of the low 21-day sensitivity: 0.193 for
  A-fixed in the robustness default cell (`robustness.json`), and 0.166 in `ATTRIBUTION.md`.
- Section 5 adds a matched-`k` arm. Whether it helps at matched false alarms on autocorrelated
  composites is left open; this spec does not assume it.

## 5. Detectors

Every detector reads the same observed contract: indicators, `U_jt`, usage, the policy log and logged
propensities. None sees `D`. `h` is retuned per detector and per `phi_D` cell on S (tuning seed) to 1
false alarm per 6 tracked person-months.

- **A-fixed, `k = 0.5`.** The frozen reference.
- **A-fixed, matched `k`.** `k* = c(delta_min) / 2`.
  - `c` is the composite shift per unit of `delta_D`, estimated help-off on the tuning seed before
    scoring.
  - It is labelled "design-known", like `phi` in section 7, because it is estimated from DR at a known
    shift.
  - The design check, run with help on, gives about 0.35, so `k*` is about 0.175.
- **Oracle.** The generator's level component. An upper bound only.

**Deferred to a follow-up (003b): the elapsed-time filter of sections 11.2 and 13.1.4.** Its form is not
fixed, and it needs its own oracle check under `triggered` and `mixed` before it can serve as a
baseline. Running it unfixed would put an unreviewed estimator into the comparison.

## 6. Baseline length and baseline probes

Effective size of the true baseline mean of `D` under AR(1), exact (asymptotic in brackets):

| baseline | `phi_D` 0.3 | 0.7 | 0.9 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 28 days | 15.4 (15.1) | 5.5 (4.9) | 2.2 (1.5) |
| 56 days | 30.5 (30.2) | 10.4 (9.9) | 3.5 (2.9) |

The layer selects persons whose baseline happened to be low (design check, synthetic). S persons who
alarm have a true baseline mean `D` of -0.168 at 0.7, against +0.119 for those who do not. At 0.9 the
figures are -0.368 and +0.268. The baseline probe block reads the same low baseline.

**Arms, 2 x 2.** Baseline length is 28 or 56 days. Baseline probes are 4 or 8, evenly spaced inside the
baseline. Baseline length sets both the detector's baseline and the window that holds the probe block. It
therefore also changes which persons alarm, and `h` is retuned per arm. The frozen arm is 28 days with 4 probes. With 8 probes the contrast SD falls from 0.913 to
`sqrt(1.429/3 + 1.429/8) = 0.809`. The factorial separates the effect of more baseline days from the
effect of more baseline probes.

At `phi_D = 0.9` no arm is claimed to solve high persistence.

## 7. Confirmation probes

**Rule.** After an alarm on day `a`, probes go out on `a + L`, `a + 2L` and `a + 3L`. The alarm is
confirmed if the mean of the answered probes, minus the baseline-block probe mean, is at least
`delta_min / 2 = 0.5`.
- With fewer than 2 answered probes, the alarm is "unconfirmable". This is reported, and not counted as
  unconfirmed.
- Probes are not replaced after non-response.

**Spacing `L`.** `L(phi) = min{L : phi^L < 0.05}`: 3, 9 and 29 days at `phi_D` 0.3, 0.7 and 0.9 (spec
001 section 15).
- The 0.05 cut is set by the selected excursion. S alarms fire at a mean `D_t` of 1.28 at 0.7 (design
  check), and `1.28 x 0.05 = 0.06`, about an eighth of the margin.
- The primary arm uses the generator's `phi_D` and is labelled "design-known persistence". It stands
  for a validation study that sizes its spacing from a pilot.
- A second arm, "fixed spacing", uses `L = 9` in every cell. In the 0.9 cell it under-spaces
  (`0.9^9 = 0.39`), and the arm reports that cost.
- Estimating `phi` from 28 baseline days is not admitted. At an effective size of 2.2, `phi` cannot be
  estimated.

**References.**
- **Baseline block** (all arms).
- **Oracle reference**, in simulation only. It uses the same baseline-block contrast with the true
  baseline `D` replaced by the generator mean 0, and the block's probe noise kept. The gap between the
  two references is therefore baseline `D` error only.
- **Comparators:** the next-day rule with 3 consecutive probes (as M2x3 in `ATTRIBUTION.md`), and the
  frozen 7-day rule.

**Design check** (synthetic; margin 0.5; A-fixed; 28 days and 4 probes; DR-1.0 counts persons detected
with no pre-onset alarm). Confirmed share:

| `phi_D` | S next day | S spaced | S spaced, oracle ref. | DR-1.0 next day | DR-1.0 spaced | S / DR alarms |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.3 | 0.415 | 0.329 | 0.293 | 0.635 | 0.626 | 441 / 222 |
| 0.7 | 0.591 | 0.335 | 0.263 | 0.855 | 0.774 | 391 / 186 |
| 0.9 | 0.826 | 0.469 | 0.293 | 0.935 | 0.812 | 386 / 154 |

- **Memory.** Spacing removes it. Against the oracle reference, S's next-day share of 0.575 and 0.762 at
  0.7 and 0.9 falls to 0.263 and 0.293. With 0.293 at 0.3, that is roughly flat across `phi_D`. DR keeps
  0.91 of its next-day share at 0.7.
- **Baseline error.** What remains above the oracle-reference level is baseline `D` error: +0.036 at
  0.3, +0.072 at 0.7 and +0.176 at 0.9.

**Cost.** Days to confirmation rise to `3L`: 9, 27 and 87 days. That is the price of the sustained
target (spec 001 section 15).

**Budget.** 3 probes per alarm, plus the baseline block. At the frozen false-alarm rate this stays far
under 3.0 per person-month (M2x3 used 0.46). The budget is reported per family and arm.

## 8. The policy log, primary separator of person from observer change

**The record.** One entry per change in the agent's own behavior. The agent writes it, and it asks
nothing of the person. Each entry holds:
- the timestamp;
- the field that changed: check-in rate, prompt schedule, model or version, help propensity, or probe
  schedule;
- the indicator map: the indicators the agent declares the change can touch;
- the trigger: *exogenous* (configuration, vendor update) or *endogenous* (caused by the layer's own
  output, with the alarm or statistic that caused it).

**Rules.** Rules L1 and L2 change the detector. L0+label leaves the detector alone and only labels its
alarms. In each case the rule acts at a logged exogenous change.

| rule | what it does | scored by |
|---|---|---|
| L0 | ignores the log: A-fixed without (iv), the unlogged reference | FA rate per family, sensitivity |
| L0+label | L0's alarms, each labelled *observer* if a logged exogenous change whose map is non-empty falls in `[a - W, a]`, and *person* otherwise. `W = 14`, matching the refractory period and the re-baseline window; `W = 28` is reported as the alternative. The rule reads only the past log | the person-labelled share per family, as A6 |
| L1 | the frozen reset (iv) on every indicator: `S` reset, all indicators re-baselined over 14 days, `S` held | FA rate per family, sensitivity |
| L2 | mapped re-baseline: only the mapped indicators leave the composite for 14 days and are re-baselined, while `S` keeps running on the rest | FA rate per family, sensitivity |
| oracle | the true observer shift subtracted | FA rate per family, sensitivity |

**Scoring.** Every alarm is scored against the section 2 status, never against a rule's label.

- *Separation* for L1, L2 and the oracle is the ratio of each PS family's false-alarm rate to S's, read
  beside DR sensitivity. For L0+label, it is DR's person-labelled share minus each PS family's.
- Spaced confirmation (section 7) can follow any rule. "Person-confirmed" means person-labelled, or
  surviving the rule, and then confirmed.
- **DR+PS.** The reference label is "person". Alarms in `[change day, t0)` were caused by the observer
  change. They are tallied as observer-caused false alarms in their own row. Sensitivity for DR+PS uses
  `exclude_pre_onset_alarms=False`, so these alarms do not zero a detection that follows. Any comparison
  with DR-1.0 also reports DR-1.0 under `False`. A pre-onset alarm still starts the 14-day refractory
  period and can delay or suppress a detection; that is part of the observer change's cost.

**Endogenous changes (PS-endo) never trigger a reset or a label.** A reset would erase the change that
caused them, and the triggering alarm is already on record. Confirmation is by probes only, as in section
7. PS-endo's false alarms after the change are reported descriptively, with no prediction.

**Stress arms.**
- A misspecified map, where the log omits indicator 6.
- Logging coverage of 1.0, against the family default of 0.5.

**Why the log is the primary separator.**
- In the frozen run, removing (iv) raised logged-AV false alarms 1.85-fold (P3).
- In `ATTRIBUTION.md`, the indicator-pattern test kept 0.87 to 0.93 of alarms in every family.
- Also in `ATTRIBUTION.md`, three next-day probes read 0.46 to 0.56 of non-person alarms as person
  change.

**Known limits.**
- Indicators alone cannot separate an unlogged PS-prop: by the invariance argument it gives the same
  indicator means as person change. The source is Meredith 1993, *Psychometrika* 58(4):525-543, checked
  on Crossref in the attribution lane (`ATTRIBUTION-PLAN.md`, commit `ccbcf51`).
- Spaced probes can separate it in simulation, because `D` is stationary there, at the confirmation
  costs of section 7.
- The log covers only changes the agent records.
- DR+PS onsets inside a re-baseline window are lost to L1, and partly to L2. That cost is a metric.

## 9. Timing (spec 001 section 12.7): recommendation

**Recommendation: timing is not part of this study's target.** A change in when the person is active,
with `D`'s mean unchanged, is null: PH and every schedule shift are scored as null. This is the first row
of the 12.6 table, "the person's mean change". A joint family, where `D` shifts and also delays activity,
is positive through `D`. When study 004 runs R2, it is read on that row, under 004's own non-inferiority
margin.

Why:
1. **Consistency.** Section 15 already holds PH null. A timing target would reopen that decision
   indirectly.
2. **Confounding by the observer.** In real traces timing moves with timezone, daylight saving, the
   agent's scheduler and autonomous lanes. The pilot attributes 0.7497 of late-window entries to
   autonomous sessions (`pilot/RESULTS.md`). A timing target would score these observer and clock changes
   as positives, which is the confusion Q3 exists to remove.
3. **Scope of any later real study.** A timing-regime target carried into a consented study would be a
   daily-schedule detector, closer to monitoring than a mean-level target (the parallel of section 15's
   privacy reasoning).
4. **The generator cannot express it yet.** The daily generator has no phase separate from amplitude
   (section 12.1).

**Sequencing.**
- The event-time generator and arms R0 to R2 (12.3 to 12.6) move to study 004, which inherits this
  target.
- 12.7's other open parameters stay open for 004: the boundary, the shift bound, the PH size, `n_min`,
  the flatness alpha and the margin.
- Timing summaries (12.5) may be reported in 004 as their own quantity, never as a state claim.

**Reversible** the same way as section 15: with a dated note before 004's freeze, or as tagged exploratory
rescoring afterwards.

## 10. Metrics

These are spec 001 section 5's metrics plus the items below. Each is reported per family, cell, detector
and arm, with paired person-bootstrap SEs.
- Sensitivity at 21 and 42 days, RMST censored at 42 days, and a chance level from pseudo-onsets on S,
  all by `delta`.
- The sub-threshold alarm rate (DR-0.5, after onset).
- Confirmation:
  - confirmed PPV against the section 2 status;
  - confirmed false alarms per person-month;
  - days to confirmation;
  - the unconfirmable share;
  - in simulation only, the oracle-reference confirmed share.
- False alarms broken down by alarm-day `D_t`, in simulation only (spec 001 section 15).
- Baseline error: the true baseline mean of `D` among alarmed and non-alarmed S persons, per baseline
  arm.
- Separation and the observer-caused tally, per section 8.
- Policy-log cost: DR+PS sensitivity for onsets inside the re-baseline window, L1 against L2.

## 11. Candidate predictions (hypotheses; thresholds fixed in `PREREG-003` after review)

`PREREG-003` restates every threshold on the new defaults, which are help-off with onsets U[70, 120],
using a non-evaluation seed and the planned SEs. H1, H2 and H3 are sized there. H2's second clause and
H3 have no design evidence yet.

**Predictions:**

- **H2.** At `phi_D = 0.9`, with 28 days and 4 probes, S's spaced confirmed share against the baseline
  block exceeds its oracle-reference share by at least 0.10. Design check: +0.176. A 56-day baseline
  shrinks that gap by at least 0.05 with the probe count held at 4.
- **H3.** On logged PS-sub and PS-use persons, L1 and L2 bring false alarms within 1.5x of S. L0 does
  not.
- **H6.** Matched `k` against `k = 0.5` at matched false alarms. No direction is predicted; the result
  is reported as an estimate with its interval.

**Construction checks.** These are labelled in the paper as checks, not findings:

- **H1.** At `phi_D = 0.7`, spaced confirmation lowers S's confirmed share by at least 0.15 against the
  next-day rule, and keeps at least 0.85 of DR-1.0's next-day share. Design check: -0.256 and 0.91 at
  about 186 DR persons. AR(1) decay makes the first part close to true by construction.
- **H4.** On DR+PS, L2 loses less sensitivity than L1 for onsets inside the re-baseline window. L2 keeps
  the unmapped indicators running.
- **H5** (a known answer). For unlogged PS-prop, no indicator-only rule (L0+label, M1) separates it from
  DR-1.0 by more than 0.10 in person-attributed share.
