# Do the observer-step claims replicate on fresh populations?

**Status: DESIGN STAGE, EXPLORATORY. Every number is synthetic. No prediction is scored.** This checks whether
the claims STEP-003, UNLOG-003 and BREAK-003 drew from one simulated population hold in new ones, and whether
their person-bootstrap intervals describe population-to-population variation. It decides nothing and changes no
spec.

Code: `sim/rep_003.py` -> `sim/rep_003.json`. `phi_D` 0.7, 28-day baseline, W 14, NET-003's DR+PS pair,
in-window persons, detectors and tuning (`tuning_003.json`) as in BREAK-003. Every imported file is unchanged.

## Why

NET-003 through BREAK-003 (next10 to next17) all ran on one population: design seed 20261003, N 1000, 247
in-window persons, one resample matrix. Each phase chose its question after reading the previous phase's numbers.
So the observer-step story the paper would tell (L1_did's transfer cost, L1_step's gain growing with rho, the
w/p break-even) is an adaptive chain fitted to one draw. Two things are unknown:
1. Whether the claims hold in fresh populations from the same generator, or partly reflect the design draw.
2. Whether the person-bootstrap intervals used throughout are calibrated. The generator draws every random
   quantity per person (AR(1) panels, loadings `lam`, events, usage), so persons are independent and the
   bootstrap should be roughly right. That has not been checked, and p* is clipped at 0 and 1, so its percentile
   intervals can be degenerate (BREAK-003 reports [1, 1] cells).

A simulation study for the paper needs this anyway: point estimates with Monte Carlo standard errors over
replicate populations, not one draw (Morris, White & Crowther 2019, ADEMP).

### Calibration of the person bootstrap

There are 408 distinct continuous quantities (T0, F0, Ts, Fs, U). The 720 planned cells repeat some of them:
F0 does not depend on rho or the arm, Fs does not depend on rho, and T0 and U(w, 0) do not depend on the arm.
- Over the 720 cells, median coverage is 0.945 and median SD / mean SE is 0.999. Over the 408 distinct
  quantities, the figures are 0.945 and 1.003, with 10th to 90th percentiles of SD / SE 0.93 to 1.08.
  Coverage's Monte Carlo SE at R = 200 is about 0.015.
- At 42 and 63 d, 1 of 272 distinct quantities has coverage below 0.90 (0.89: k*, 42 d, `conc-0.5`, rho 0,
  U(2, 1), mean 0.053).
- 18 distinct quantities have coverage below 0.90 (lowest 0.775). 17 of them are at 21 d and 17 at k*. Every one
  has a mean within 0.06 of 0, and 16 are within 0.015, which is about 4 of 270 in-window persons. In 16 of the
  18, SD / SE is above 1 (up to 1.27), so the bootstrap SE is too small there. A likely reason, not shown here,
  is that these contrasts rest on a few discordant persons. Of the 52 distinct 21 d quantities with |mean| up to
  0.015, 16 fall below 0.90: 15 of 42 at k* and 1 of 10 at k 0.5.
- UNLOG-003's L1_did - L1_step added share: its own 12 cells (`conc0.5`, `post0.5`) cover at 0.905 to 0.965. The
  6 `conc-0.5` cells, an arm UNLOG-003 did not have, cover at 0.935 to 0.97, except 0.73 at k*, 21 d (mean -0.004).
- p*'s coverage against the 200-population mean is below 0.90 in 92 of 216 cells. 89 of those are cells where
  the generator p* (the plug-in value from the mean U(w, 0) and U(w, 1)) is clipped at 1 (79) or 0 (10). There
  the mean of the per-population p* sits just inside the clip, and [1, 1] or [0, 0] intervals miss it by
  construction (rho 1, p*(1), k 0.5, 63 d, `conc0.5`: mean 0.9996, coverage 0.145). Where the generator p* is
  interior, 51 of 54 cells cover at 0.90 or above (the 3 below are k*, 21 d, w 4). Coverage against the mean is
  the wrong check for a clipped quantity.

So for the L1_step - L1 TPR, FPR and U contrasts checked here (DR+PS, `phi_D` 0.7) at 42 and 63 d, the person-
bootstrap intervals are close to nominal (median coverage 0.945; 1 of 272 below 0.90). Some 21 d contrasts near 0,
mostly at k*, are not (16 of 52 below 0.90, as low as 0.775). p*'s intervals are fine where p* is interior. Where
it is clipped they are degenerate, and this check cannot score them. Nothing else used from NET-003 to BREAK-003
was tested.

1. **The design draw overstated L1's no-change false-alarm excess.** F0 (L1_step - L1 FPR without the shift)
   is -0.093 in the design and -0.069 (0.002) over the 200 populations; the design value sits at the 15.5th
   percentile. F0 enters every U(w, 0) with weight w and does not depend on rho. The design's part A gain is
   above the generator mean by 0.044, 0.040, 0.039 and 0.024 at rho 0, 0.5, 1 and 2. F0's draw contributes 0.024
   at every rho; T0's draw contributes the rest (the design value sits at the 80th, 70th, 70th and 50th
   percentiles). This is one draw's sampling error, not a bias in the method.
2. **At rho 0, L1_step's D-dependent gain is about zero in the generator:** 0.004 (0.002), with the point
   above 0 in 54% of populations. STEP-003's rho-0 value of 0.049 was mostly the draw. The gain needs rho > 0.
3. **BREAK-003's failed expectation 2 was the draw, not the generator.** U(1, 1) at rho 0 is -0.028 in the design
   (1st percentile) and +0.041 (0.002) in the generator, positive in 91% of populations. Under Youden weighting,
   L1_step's U is at least L1's at p = 1 in the generator at every rho in the sweep, as BREAK-003 expectation 2 predicted.
   The generator p*(1) is therefore 1 at every rho, but at rho 0 that rests on U(1, 0) = 0.004 (MCSE 0.002). The
   per-population p*(1) at rho 0 is bimodal (mean 0.54, SD 0.49): with U(1, 0) about 0, p* is 0 or 1 by the sign
   of a near-zero contrast, so p* is ill-conditioned there.
4. **The break-even's ordering in rho holds. Its level at w = 4 is lower than the design's.** The design's p*(4) was
   0.06 to 0.08 above the per-population mean at every rho (design percentiles 0.72 to 0.75), mostly through the
   shared F0. p*(2) differs by at most 0.07 (rho 0 and rho 2) and is unchanged at rho 0.5 (0.574 vs 0.575).
5. **L1's win at w = 2 is small from rho 1.** U(2, 1) is -0.061 at rho 1 and -0.047 at rho 2, and the population's
   own interval excludes 0 in 16% and 9% of populations.
