# DR+PS sensitivity net of a no-change counterfactual

**Status: DESIGN STAGE, EXPLORATORY. Every number is synthetic. No prediction is scored.** This informs
PREREG-003's H4 wording and the L2-against-oracle reading. It decides nothing and changes no spec.

Code: `sim/net_003.py` -> `sim/net_003.json`. Design seed 20261003, `phi_D` 0.7, 28-day baseline, the design
run's resample matrix. `study003.py`, `decide_003.py`, `drift_sim.py` and spec 003 are unchanged.

## Why

On logged DR+PS persons with onset in the re-baseline window, L2's 21-day sensitivity sits above the oracle
rule's: 0.267 against 0.227 at W = 14 (`design_003.json`), 0.312 at W = 21 (`window_003.json`). The oracle rule
subtracts the true observer shift, so it should be an upper reference for a rule that only approximates that
subtraction. No paired L2-minus-oracle difference was computed (DECIDE-003, WINDOW-003 open items).

A sensitivity on DR+PS counts any alarm in `[t0, t0 + 21)`. Some of those alarms could happen without the
change in `D`, for three reasons:
1. Chance. S's chance level at 21 days is 0.044 (DESIGN-003).
2. The observer change. The change day `c` lies within 5 days of `t0`, and day `c` is scored unheld.
3. L2's 3-indicator composite during the hold. It averages half as many z-scores, so its SD is higher, and `h`
   was tuned on the 6-indicator composite.

Raw sensitivity cannot separate these from detection. A paired counterfactual can: the same persons, seeds,
events, log and observer change, with the `D` step removed (`delta_D = 0`). The question is whether a rule's
alarms in the window depend on the change it is supposed to detect.

## Plan (committed before the run)

For W in (14, 21), detectors A-fixed `k = 0.5`, A-fixed `k*` and oracle-D, and rules L0, L1, L2 and oracle:
1. Raw sensitivity (the design's), chance (the same rule on the counterfactual), and net = raw - chance per
   person. Also the share of raw detections that the counterfactual also has. Horizons 21, 42 and 63 days
   from onset, `exclude_pre_onset_alarms=False` as in the design.
2. Populations: logged persons (n about 500), and those with onset in `[c, c + 13]` (H4's population, 247).
3. Paired differences at 21 days, raw and net: L2 - oracle, L2 - L1 (H4), L1 - oracle, L0 - oracle.
4. Mechanism check: the counterfactual composite's SD under L0 and L2 on hold days `(c, c + W]` against
   pre-change days `[28, c)`, in-window persons, point values.

Asserts: the DR+PS panel equals the design's; the counterfactual differs from it only by the D step, with the
same usage and log; at W = 14 each rule's raw in-window sensitivity and H4 reproduce `design_003.json`.

Expected:
- L2's chance level is above the oracle rule's, and L2's composite SD on hold days is above its pre-change SD.
- L2 - oracle shrinks on net and its interval covers 0 (raw 0.04 is about the size of the chance excess).
- L1's chance level is near 0 (S reset and held through the window), so L1's net is close to its raw value.
- H4 net is smaller than raw at both windows, and stays positive.
- L0's chance level is high (the observer change alone alarms), and L0 - oracle shrinks toward 0 on net.
- At 42 and 63 days L1 recovers little at W = 14 for in-window persons, because the re-baseline over the hold
  absorbs part of the ramp.

## Results (DESIGN STAGE, synthetic; they inform PREREG-003's wording and score nothing)

Provenance: code `net_003.py` at commit `861875e`, output `net_003.json` sha256
`55ac1467b2257b8aca86c2b6958650e7758625016aae4c6c4456095eebdc6237`. A second run reproduced it byte for byte
(`cmp`). Every assert in the plan passed. "Only by the D step" in the plan means the step and its downstream effects in the
generator (D's loadings on the indicators, and context); the asserts check D, usage and the log. Intervals are 95% paired person-bootstrap intervals over the design
run's resample matrix, "est [lo, hi]". Populations: in-window n = 247, logged n = 496.

Terms. The JSON's `chance` is the **no-change rate**: the rule's in-window detection rate on the counterfactual.
It lumps the three causes listed under Why; for L0 it is mostly the observer change. The JSON's `net` is the
**D-dependent sensitivity**, raw minus the no-change rate per person. The counterfactual's in-window alarms are
almost always a subset of the factual ones, so this is the share of persons whose in-window alarm exists only
with the D change. It credits D with any interaction between D and the observer shift. It also counts as zero a
person who alarms in both arms, even when D brought the alarm earlier, which works against rules with a high
no-change rate (L0, L2). It is not cumulative over horizons and can fall as the horizon grows.

### Against the expectations

1. **L2's no-change rate is above the oracle rule's: at k 0.5 only, at W = 14.** It is 0.134 against 0.101 at
   k 0.5, and 0.085 against 0.089 at k*. At W = 21, k*, it is 0.093 against 0.089. No paired interval was
   computed for these. **L2's hold-day composite SD is above its pre-change SD: yes.** On the counterfactual
   it is 0.692 on hold days against 0.581 before the change; L0's is 0.577 against 0.581; L2's at W = 21 is
   0.691. The SD does not explain the k* result, where the SD is just as high and the no-change rate is not.
2. **L2 - oracle shrinks and covers 0 when D-dependent: yes.** Every D-dependent interval covers 0; at k 0.5,
   W = 21, the raw one did not. No L2 advantage over the oracle rule is detectable once the D change is
   isolated, though the intervals do not rule one out. The no-change rates account for 0.033 of the 0.041
   raw gap at W = 14, and 0.049 of 0.085 at W = 21.
3. **L1's no-change rate is near 0: no at W = 14, k 0.5.** It is 0.057, half of L1's raw 0.113. It is 0.004 at
   k* and 0.004 at W = 21, k 0.5. At W = 21, L1 detects 1 of 247 in-window persons under k 0.5, and that
   person also alarms without the change. It detects none under k*. D-dependent is 0.000 under both.
4. **D-dependent H4 is smaller than raw and stays positive: yes,** at both windows and both detectors. It is
   0.50 to 0.63 of raw. The no-change rate cancels only partly in L2 - L1, because L1's is lower than L2's.
5. **L0's no-change rate is high: yes** (0.251). **L0 - oracle shrinks toward 0: partly.** It halves at k 0.5,
   and its interval still excludes 0. At k*, the D-dependent interval covers 0. A likely reason, not tested:
   the effects do not add. The observer shift brings S closer to `h`, so the D change tips more alarms under L0
   than without the shift, and the D-dependent measure credits that to D.
6. **L1 recovers little at 42 and 63 days (W = 14, in-window): partly.** At k 0.5, L1's D-dependent
   sensitivity is 0.057, 0.186 and 0.190 at 21, 42 and 63 days. The oracle rule's is 0.126, 0.332 and 0.441,
   and L2's is 0.134, 0.267 and 0.344. So L1 gains between 21 and 42 days and is flat after. Its raw
   sensitivity keeps rising (0.377 to 0.494), matched by later counterfactual alarms (0.190 to 0.304). L1 runs
   at 0.45, 0.56 and 0.43 of the oracle's D-dependent value. At W = 21: 0.000, 0.121 and 0.154. The reason
   given in the plan (the re-baseline over the hold absorbs part of the ramp) was not tested.

### What this changes for PREREG-003 (proposals, not decisions)

1. **A DR+PS sensitivity needs its no-change rate beside it.** The design reports DR+PS sensitivities with
   `exclude_pre_onset_alarms=False`. At W = 14, 21 days, in-window, their no-change rates are 0.085 to 0.279
   for L0, L2 and the oracle rule, and 0.004 to 0.057 for L1. DESIGN-003's S chance level of 0.044 does not
   apply to them. It is computed with the default `True`, and on S, which has no observer change. oracle-D
   isolates the flag, since it ignores the observer change: 0.076 on S with `True` (DESIGN-003), 0.130 here
   with `False`.
   - Proposal: score H4 and every DR+PS rule comparison as D-dependent, against the paired `delta_D = 0`
     counterfactual, with raw reported beside it. The counterfactual exists only in simulation; this run gives
     no real-data analogue.
2. **Drop the reading "L2 beats the oracle rule".** DESIGN-003, DECIDE-003 (item 4: "Not expected", point
   estimate only) and WINDOW-003 reported it beside the oracle without a no-change rate. None of them claimed
   it as a finding.
3. **The W = 21 option:** at 21 days, L1's in-window raw sensitivity is 0.004 and its D-dependent value is 0. By
   63 days the D-dependent value is 0.154, against the oracle rule's 0.441 (expectations 3 and 6). This adds to
   the cost recorded in WINDOW-003, and the decision stays his.
